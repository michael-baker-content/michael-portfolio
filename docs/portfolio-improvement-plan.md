## 2. Collect professional examples before rewriting Experience

Start with two or three examples from enterprise production, editorial systems, or accessible learning content. Use only material that can be shared. An anonymized account can be useful without naming a client or reproducing confidential work.

For each example, record:

- **Context:** What kind of content, audience, and publishing environment was involved?
- **Problem:** What concrete inconsistency, accessibility issue, review bottleneck, or production constraint needed attention?
- **Responsibility:** What did Michael personally review, change, coordinate, or deliver? What belonged to other people?
- **Action:** What decision or process change addressed the problem?
- **Evidence:** What shareable artifact, documented result, or firsthand observation supports the account?
- **Result and limits:** What happened? If no measured result is available, describe the delivered work without inventing an improvement percentage.
- **Publication boundary:** What details must be generalized or omitted?

Completion criteria: two approved examples with concrete actions and supported results, ready to adapt into a short Experience section. Until then, retain the existing responsibilities overview.

## Suggested sequence

1. Finish the current content-and-screenshot release using the separate release checklist.
2. Collect professional examples and first-priority product evidence.
3. Revise the overlapping case studies around their distinct editorial jobs.
4. Add the supported Experience examples and decision-focused images.
5. Review home, index, and detail pages together on desktop and phone widths. Check whether a visitor can identify Michael's contribution and find related work without reading every case study.

Do not infer learning gains, retention, audience growth, or improved usability from implementation or screenshots. Record observations and measured results only when that evidence exists.
